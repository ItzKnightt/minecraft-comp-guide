# Getting Into PvP

You have the hardware, the mods, and the settings. Here's how to actually
get good — without burning out.

## Pick a gamemode and main it

Don't split time across five modes. Pick **one** and commit for at least a
few weeks:

- **Practice/duels servers** — fastest feedback loop for pure mechanics
  (aim, strafing, hits).
- **Ranked modes** — teaches game sense: positioning, timing, when to
  fight or flee.
- **Pot PvP / SMP fights** — teaches resource management and composure.

A good starting split: 60% practice server for mechanics, 40% "real"
matches where the pressure is on.

## Keep improving deliberately

Random play makes you plateau. Structure your sessions:

1. **Warm up** — 10–15 minutes on a practice server before ranked.
2. **Isolate one weakness** — e.g. "I keep losing trades when they jump".
   Spend a session only working on that.
3. **Review losses** — record clips. Most losses come from 2–3 repeatable
   mistakes, not "bad RNG".
4. **Don't chase clicks** — more CPS is not the win condition past a
   threshold; clean strafing and timing beat spam.

## Tools for feedback

- Record with a lightweight recorder (OBS replay buffer, or a mod with
  built-in recording).
- Watch your own fights at 0.5× speed — you'll spot the mistake the
  moment it happens.
- Some servers have built-in replay; use it.

## The realistic timeline

- **Week 1–2:** settings feel comfortable, aim is "meh" — this is normal.
- **Month 1:** mechanics click; you win fights you used to lose.
- **Month 3+:** game sense starts dominating; wins come from decisions
  more than clicks.

Progress is not linear. A bad session means nothing; a bad month of
deliberate practice means a lot.
