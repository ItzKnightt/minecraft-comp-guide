# Mouse, Mousepad & Keyboard

These are the parts your aim and movement physically pass through. Priority
order: mouse first, mousepad second, keyboard last (any decent keyboard
works for Minecraft; mice and aim are far more personal).

## Mouse

- **Shape over specs.** A mouse you can grip comfortably for hours beats a
  spec sheet. Lightweight (under ~75 g) helps low-sensitivity players flick
  and track without fatigue. The Glorious Model O is a long-time community
  favorite for exactly this reason.
- **Sensor:** any modern optical sensor (PixArt 33xx/39xx class) is
  effectively flawless. Don't pay for "better" sensors.
- **Polling rate:** 1000 Hz is fine. 4K/8K Hz is not worth it for Minecraft —
  the gains are marginal and it eats CPU headroom your FPS wants.
- **Buttons:** at least two thumb buttons for hotbar swapping / drop-item
  binds (see [Keybinds](../controls/keybinds.html)).

## Mousepad

- **Go big.** Low-sensitivity PvP players swing their arm; a large or XXL
  pad gives you the room. Even medium-sens players benefit from a wide pad.
- **Surface:** cloth for control and a consistent glide; hard pads for
  faster flicks. Cloth is the safe default.
- Keep it clean — a dirty cloth pad changes your glide noticeably.

## Keyboard

- **Switches:** linear (red-style) switches are the standard for fast
  repeated taps (W-tap, block clutching). Tactile is fine too — it's mostly
  preference.
- **Analog / hall-effect keyboards:** rapid-trigger style input (e.g.
  Wooting-style keyboards, configured in Wootility) lets you re-trigger a
  key the instant it moves, which is a real advantage for strafing and
  W-tapping. If you're buying new, this is the one genuine upgrade worth
  considering.
- **Anti-ghosting / N-key rollover** so holding W + A + a bind never drops
  inputs.
- Anything mechanical and wired is good enough — don't let keyboards eat
  your monitor budget.
